FullCircle

Nurse Scheduling Platform with StaffQuery, a Contract and Policy Assistant

Fair to nurses, defensible for managers, citation backed for both.

FullCircle is one product with two surfaces: a scheduling platform and a contract and policy assistant, both grounded in the same corpus of union contract and staffing policy documents. The schedule and the answers agree with each other because they read from the same source.

Manager surface

Roster upload by CSV, availability collection, constraint aware monthly schedule generation, live coverage and gap tracking, time off and swap handling, and publishing.

Nurse surface

Sign in to view your schedule, submit availability, and request time off or swaps. StaffQuery, a mobile first assistant, answers contract and policy questions in plain language, with citations to document and page.

The hard technical problem: where a hospital policy conflicts with the union contract, the contract takes precedence. That precedence rule has to be encoded in how the system retrieves and answers, not left for the model to guess.

What the pilot actually was: StaffQuery is the surface that has been in front of nurses, not the scheduling platform. I put a working prototype in front of frontline nurses on my unit and watched what they asked it. The suggested questions in the product came straight from those sessions: overtime rules, how much notice a schedule change requires, call back pay, and what counts as hours worked. That is the point of the pilot. It was clinician user research, not an adoption number.

Putting it in front of real nurses is also what showed me the distance to production: a security review, clear PHI boundaries, a governed source of truth for policy documents, versioning for contracts that get renegotiated, hardened authentication, and a real evaluation method for whether an answer is correct.

I built StaffQuery end to end myself, a working RAG prototype made while teaching myself to code. The staffing solver and the rest of FullCircle were co-built with my team. The scheduling platform has not been piloted yet and we are looking for a first site. Alongside the build we developed the go-to-market strategy, mapped California regulatory exposure under AB 394 and SB 596, and presented to Stanford faculty, clinical partners, and nursing leadership.

About Me

The person behind the credentials.

I came to nursing the long way. My first degree was in biology and chemistry with a sociology minor, and NCAA cross country and track along the way. I didn't really understand what nurses did until I worked with them. Then I realized they carry a huge part of care delivery and are essentially healthcare's operations champions. Nursing school in Michigan, night shifts in trauma and neuro ICUs, and eventually a move west to Palo Alto.

The thread has always been thinking critically, learning, and fixing systems. I precepted new ICU nurses, chaired unit committees, and built shadowing programs. Along the way I learned that most challenges sort into buckets of people, process, or technology, and putting a problem in the right bucket is often how you find its root cause. The distance runner mindset never left either. I've finished the Boston Marathon three times (2018, 2023, and 2024), and I'm at my best on long, patient problems that reward showing up every day.

This site is one of those problems. I built it the same way I built FullCircle, by shipping, with AI as my pair programmer.
